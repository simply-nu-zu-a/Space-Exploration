Planet

VENUS

Nicknamed "The Veiled Golden Inferno" because its brilliant pale-yellow sulfuric clouds conceal a crushing atmosphere where runaway greenhouse forces trap heat up to 465°C, making it the brightest yet most apocalyptic world in Sol. Earth's twin in size and composition, transformed into an apocalyptic furnace by an unchecked runaway greenhouse effect and retrograde rotation. Enveloped in dense clouds of sulfuric acid with surface atmospheric pressures 92 times Earth's (equivalent to 900 meters underwater). Supercritical carbon dioxide fluid covers vast basaltic lava plains, where temperatures soar to 465°C—hotter than Mercury—with a day longer than its orbital year.

SOLAR TIDAL DISRUPTION

Venus possesses zero moons. Like Mercury, its proximity to the Sun and strong solar gravitational tides make it nearly impossible for satellites to maintain long-term orbital stability.

Additionally, Venus's slow retrograde rotation means that any moon orbiting within its sphere of influence would experience tidal deceleration, causing it to spiral inward and crash into the crushing Venusian atmosphere.

✦ 01. The Runaway Greenhouse Furnace

Venus is the hottest planet in the Solar System despite orbiting twice as far from the Sun as Mercury. Its thick atmosphere of 96.5% Carbon Dioxide acts as an impenetrable thermal blanket, trapping solar infrared radiation and driving global surface temperatures to a relentless +465°C (+869°F) across the entire planet day and night.

At the surface, atmospheric pressure reaches 92 Bar (9.2 MPa)—equivalent to the crushing hydrostatic force found 900 meters deep in Earth's oceans. Under these extreme conditions, carbon dioxide behaves as a supercritical fluid, flowing over rocks with liquid-like density.

✦ 02. Volcanic Continents & Lava Channels

Over 80% of Venus's surface is paved in vast volcanic basalt plains and fractured tectonic highlands like Ishtar Terra and Aphrodite Terra. Radar mapping by the Magellan orbiter revealed over 1,600 major volcanoes, pancake domes, and circular tectonic crowns known as Coronae.

Its highest mountain range, Maxwell Montes, rises over +11 Kilometers above the average surface radius. In this high-altitude mountain air, radar scans discovered mysterious metallic 'snow' deposits believed to be bismuth or telluride condensates coating the volcanic peaks.

✦ 03. Acid Cloud Decks & Super-Rotation

Venus is perpetually hidden beneath a 20-kilometer-thick shroud of highly reflective clouds composed of concentrated droplets of Sulfuric Acid (H₂SO₄). These clouds reflect 70% of sunlight into space, making Venus the third brightest object in Earth's sky after the Sun and Moon.

While the solid planet spins sluggishly, its upper atmosphere is locked in Super-Rotation: high-altitude jet streams whip around the entire globe in just four Earth days at speeds exceeding 360 km/h (224 mph), transferring heat from the equator to the poles.

✦ 04. Retrograde Clockwise Rotation

Venus is one of only two planets in Sol that exhibits Retrograde Rotation: looking down from the North Pole, Venus rotates clockwise on its axis with an axial tilt of 177.36° (effectively upside down). On Venus, the Sun rises in the west and sets in the east.

It takes 243.02 Earth Days for Venus to complete a single sidereal rotation—meaning a single Venusian sidereal day is longer than its entire orbital year (224.70 Earth Days). A solar day from one sunrise to the next lasts 116.75 Earth days.
