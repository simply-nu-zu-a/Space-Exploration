URANUS

Known as "The Cyan Ice Giant" because atmospheric methane gas selectively absorbs red light wavelengths, scattering a serene, ethereal aquamarine-cyan glow across its icy mantle. An extraordinary ice giant rolling on its side along its orbital plane, featuring a cyan methane atmosphere, chaotic magnetic tumble, and 13 narrow dark rings. Knocked onto an extreme 97.77° axial tilt by an ancient protoplanetary impact, each pole experiences 42 years of continuous sunlight followed by 42 years of darkness. Plunging to -224°C—the coldest recorded planetary atmosphere in Sol—it conceals a supercritical water-ammonia ice mantle surrounding a small rocky core.

MIRANDA

Miranda boasts the most bizarre, extreme geological topography in the Solar System. It features colossal patchwork coronae and Verona Rupes—the tallest cliff face known, dropping vertically over 20 kilometers into jagged icy canyons.

Scientists hypothesize Miranda was repeatedly shattered by giant impacts during its early formation and gravitationally reassembled into a chaotic patchwork of silicate rock and water ice.

✦ 01. The Extreme 98° Sideways Tilt

Uranus is unique among all planets in having an axial obliquity of 97.77°—its rotational axis is tilted almost completely parallel to the plane of the Solar System. Rather than spinning upright like a top, Uranus literally rolls like a ball along its 84-year orbital path around the Sun.

This extreme sideways orientation—likely caused by a cataclysmic collision with an Earth-sized protoplanet during early Solar System formation—causes bizarre seasonal extremes: each pole endures 42 continuous Earth years of unbroken daylight followed by 42 years of freezing polar darkness.

✦ 02. The Coldest Atmosphere & Cyan Sky

Uranus holds the undisputed record for the coldest atmosphere recorded on any major Solar System planet, dipping to a frigid -224°C (49 K). Unlike gas giant cousins Jupiter and Saturn, Uranus possesses very little internal convective core heat, radiating almost zero excess geothermal energy into space.

Composed of 82.5% Hydrogen, 15.2% Helium, and 2.3% Methane, methane gas in the upper atmosphere absorbs red wavelengths from incoming sunlight, scattering pristine cyan, aquamarine, and turquoise hues back into the cosmic vacuum.

✦ 03. Supercritical Ice Mantle & Tilted Core

Beneath its cloud deck lies a vast mantle comprising 80% or more of the planet's mass, composed of a hot, dense supercritical fluid of water, ammonia, and methane ices under thousands of atmospheres of pressure.

Uranus's magnetic field is extraordinary: it is tilted 59° away from the planet's rotational axis and offset from the center of the planet by a third of its radius. As Uranus spins sideways, its magnetic dipole corkscrews and tumbles wildly through interplanetary space, periodically opening and closing its magnetosphere to solar wind particles every 17 hours.

✦ 04. 13 Dark Rings & Shakespearean Moons

Uranus is encircled by 13 distinct narrow dark rings. Unlike the bright, reflective water-ice rings of Saturn, Uranian rings are composed of extremely dark boulders coated in carbonaceous radiation-processed organics with an albedo of just 2%.

The planet hosts 28 known moons named after characters from the works of William Shakespeare and Alexander Pope. Its major satellites include extreme canyon world Miranda (featuring 20-km-tall Verona Rupes cliffs), bright Ariel, dark Umbriel, and giant Titania.
