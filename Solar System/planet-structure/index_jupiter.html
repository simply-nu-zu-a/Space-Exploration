JUPITER

Crowned "The Gas Giant King" because it holds over 70% of all planetary mass in Sol, ruling a majestic miniature solar system of 95 moons with unmatched gravitational dominance. More massive than all other planets combined, Jupiter commands a mini solar system of 95 moons, turbulent ammonia jet streams, and centuries-old anticyclones. Its colossal magnetosphere—powered by a churning interior mantle of metallic hydrogen under 4 million bars of pressure—is 20,000 times stronger than Earth's. Anchored by the Great Red Spot anticyclone capable of swallowing Earth whole, it acts as Sol's gravitational shield, vacuuming up cosmic debris and comets.

GALILEAN MOON 01 // VOLCANIC INFERNO

IO

Io is the most volcanically active world in the entire Solar System, hosting over 400 active volcanoes, gigantic sulfur lava lakes, and volcanic plumes that blast gas and sulfur dioxide hundreds of kilometers into space. Its surface is constantly repaved in vibrant yellow, orange, and black sulfur compounds.

Caught in a gravitational tug-of-war between Jupiter's immense mass and orbital resonances with Europa and Ganymede, tidal friction continuously flexes Io's solid rocky crust up and down by up to 100 meters, generating massive internal geothermal heat.

✦ 01. Colossal Scale & Metallic Ocean

Jupiter is the gargantuan anchor of our outer planetary system, holding a total mass of 1.898 × 10²⁷ Kilograms (318 times the mass of Earth and 2.5 times that of all other planets in Sol combined). Large enough to contain 1,321 Earths within its volume, it lacks any solid surface crust.

Descending through its upper hydrogen-helium atmosphere, rising temperatures and immense crushing pressures gradually compress the gas into a supercritical fluid and eventually into an ocean of Liquid Metallic Hydrogen thousands of kilometers deep, conducting massive electrical currents that power a titanic magnetosphere.

✦ 02. The Great Red Spot & Jet Streams

Jupiter's vibrant visual bands are formed by opposing east-west jet streams reaching speeds of up to 540 km/h (335 mph), shearing alternating light zones (upwelling cold ammonia clouds) and dark belts (descending warm air laced with sulfur and phosphorus).

Its crowning storm is the Great Red Spot, an enormous anticyclonic vortex spanning over 16,000 Kilometers across—large enough to swallow Earth whole. Observed continuously for over 350 years, this towering high-pressure storm system rotates counterclockwise every six days.

✦ 03. Mini Solar System of Galilean Moons

Jupiter acts as a miniature planetary system with 95 confirmed natural satellites. Its four primary moons—discovered by Galileo Galilei in 1610—include volcanic Io, global ocean world Europa, magnetic titan Ganymede, and ancient cratered Callisto.

Their unique 4:2:1 Laplace Orbital Resonance causes Io, Europa, and Ganymede to gravitationally tug on one another every orbit, generating relentless tidal friction that flexes their interiors, melts subterranean rock on Io, and keeps vast saltwater oceans liquid under Europa's icy shell.

✦ 04. 10-Hour Rotation & Giant Magnetosphere

Despite its immense girth, Jupiter has the shortest day of any planet in the Solar System, completing a full sidereal rotation in just 9 Hours, 55 Minutes, 30 Seconds. This frantic rotation generates powerful centrifugal flattening, making its equatorial diameter 9,276 km wider than its poles.

Driven by its liquid metallic core, Jupiter generates a colossal magnetic field 20,000 times stronger than Earth's. Its magnetosphere extends millions of kilometers toward the Sun and stretches past Saturn's orbit, forming the largest continuous structure in the Solar System.
